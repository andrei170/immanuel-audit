The most important line in this entire document

You are both using the number $2,500. You are giving it away. They are promising to pay it.

Your Facebook ad says “up to $2,500 off a roof replacement”. Theirs says “if water gets in after we're done, we pay you $2,500” — a written no-leak guarantee.

Same number. Opposite economics. Yours costs you $2,500 on every job it wins and attracts the price shopper you told us you always lose to. Theirs costs nothing unless they fail, and it attracts the homeowner who is frightened of hiring the wrong roofer.

You said it yourself on our call: “I don't like selling on a discounted ad anyways, because we don't really do that. So it's not congruent with our full process.” Your instinct was right, and your closest competitor has already proved it.

Not one of their twenty ads leads with a discount

Every angle they run is risk reversal, speed, or transparency. These are their actual hooks:

  • Price transparency — “Tired of calling roofing companies just to hear, we'll give you a price when we get there?” Get a real range in about 30 seconds, no appointment.
  • Fear of the wrong contractor — “There's a reason roofer and scam show up in the same sentence so often.”
  • Accountability — same foreman start to finish, hourly photo updates, full cleanup.
  • Speed — “Roof replaced in one day”, run as a headline across most ads.
  • Cash-flow removal — 0 down, 0 payments, 0% interest for 12 months.
  • Cost of delay — curling shingles, soaked underlayment, one bad winter turning a cheap fix into rotted plywood.
  • Honesty positioning — “If it only needs a repair, we'll tell you. If it's fine, we'll tell you that too.”

Every one of those is available to you today at no extra cost. You already do the work; you are simply not saying any of it.

The strategic move they made four days ago

On 4 September Blue Bird launched at least eight new ads at once, and they do not point at their own website. They point at pricemyjob.com — a separate instant-estimate funnel.

The mechanic: answer a few questions about the roof, its size and what is on it, and get a real price range before anyone visits. If the range works, they book the on-site visit and lock the exact price.

That is a qualification tool. It filters out the people who were never going to spend $17,000 before a salesman is dispatched — which is precisely the multi-step qualification form we discussed on our call. Your closest competitor built it four days ago.

It also explains something. If Blue Bird are qualifying before the appointment and you are not, the unqualified traffic in your shared market has to land somewhere — and at $315 a lead with under 6% converting, some of it is landing on you.

What their ads tell you about them — and where they are beatable
  • Their proven winner has been live since 11 June — a 14-second video of a dark, curling roof torn off and replaced in a day. Three months live means it works. Two variants of it run side by side.
  • Several ads sit under 100 impressions. Those are tests, not winners. They are spending deliberately on finding the next one — which is what 20 ads and a rotation buys you.
  • One ad references the “Capital Region” — that is the Albany area, not Oneida. They are a regional operator reaching into your town, not a local firm. That is the same argument you already make about Stellar Roofing, and it works better against Blue Bird because they are actually good.
  • Their founder is named in the copy — Sergey. A founder story is one of the few things a genuinely local, long-standing business can beat an expanding regional one on.
  • Two ads mention “Kornerstone” rather than Blue Bird in the body copy. We do not know whether that is a sister brand, a partner or a copy error, and we are not going to guess — but it is worth knowing they may be running under more than one name.

Why The Leads Are Bad — It Is The Offer
The diagnosis you reached yourself on the call

Your Facebook offer is “up to $2,500 off a roof replacement”. You said it better than I could:

“When you try to hook people with up to $2,500 off a roof replacement, it's like, oh, I wonder if I could qualify for that much. Let's just see. They're not serious, because it's generic. It's not specific.”

A discount hook selects for the one customer you cannot serve. You told me the only reason people do not choose Immanuel is price — that they go with someone $2,500 to $3,000 cheaper. So an ad led by a discount is deliberately attracting the exact person who will not buy from you.

Two other things compound it. Five creatives against $5,000 of spend is creative fatigue by week two. And you said the targeting is aimed at areas you do not normally serve — so a share of that $5,000 bought leads you could never have converted.

Where The Market Is Weak — And What You Own
The genuinely good news
  • Nobody local is winning on measurement except Blue Bird. Campany's site blocks inspection, but the wider market here is behind — and that is an opportunity rather than a threat.
  • You already have the whole stack installed. GA4, Tag Manager, Google Ads and a Meta pixel. Blue Bird has the same. Nobody else obviously does. You are one configuration job away from parity with the best-run competitor in your market.
  • 85% of your work is organic — referrals, wrapped trucks, billboards, yard signs, word of mouth. That is a genuinely strong position, and it means the paid side is upside rather than life support.
  • Stellar Roofing is a Syracuse operation coming into your area, and you rate their work poorly. A large out-of-town firm with a weak local reputation is the easiest competitor to position against — it is exactly the “we live here, they don't” argument.
  • Repairs net you around 60% against 55% on replacements, and they are only 10% of your mix. That is the most under-exploited line in the business.
  • You can absorb 20 more jobs a month. Very few contractors this size have that headroom.
What We Would Do, In Order
  • 1. Attach the conversion tags — before another dollar. Nothing else can be judged until this is done
  • 2. Ask for reviews systematically — 700 customers a year against 51 reviews is the biggest free win available
  • 3. Put the rating and count on the site, with names on the testimonials
  • 4. Kill the discount hook — it is selecting for the customer you cannot win
  • 5. Fix the targeting so you stop paying for areas you do not serve
Then
  • Rebuild the creative — five ads against $5,000 guarantees fatigue
  • Lead with proof rather than price: named jobs, the warranty, financing, and being local against Syracuse
  • Push the repair line — higher margin, faster cash, and it opens replacement conversations later
  • Call tracking, so phone calls count alongside forms
  • Judge everything on cost per booked job, never clicks

That is not a budget problem. Fix the tracking, ask for the reviews, and stop advertising a discount to people who were only ever going to buy on price.
