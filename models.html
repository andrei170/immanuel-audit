We went looking for the best roofing advertisers in the state outside New York City, so you would have something to model. Every one of them turned out to be advertising in your backyard.

Comfort Windows & Doors has a page called "Oneida Roofing Company". Melo Roofing has pages for Oneida, Rome and Utica. Graves Brothers, with offices across upstate New York, has built landing pages for roofing, roof repair, siding and gutters in Utica.

So this is two documents in one: what the best in the state are doing that you can copy, and who is already spending money to reach your customers.

1 — Graves Brothers: The Google Ads System To Copy
Pages on gravesbros.com whose titles begin "LP: GAD" — 43 of them: gad-roofing-utica gad-roof-repair-utica gad-siding-utica gad-gutters-utica gad-roofing-syr gad-roof-repair-syr gad-roofing-cortland gad-roofing-binghamton gad-ice-damming-syr gad-insulation-syr gad-window-syr gad-specials ...and the same pattern for Rochester, Buffalo, Albany and beyond A different local phone number for each market, including a 315 number.
Why this is the model for your paid search

"LP" means landing page and "GAD" is how Google Ads pages are commonly labelled. Every service in every city gets its own page, so somebody who searches "roof repair Utica" lands on a page about roof repair in Utica, with a Utica-area number on it, not on a generic homepage.

That is the single biggest reason a click turns into a call. It is also a setup built to show which city and which service pays for itself, because every page and every phone number can be measured separately.

You told us you are paying $16 to $22 a click, and your Google Ads conversion tag is built but not attached, so those clicks are not reporting back what they produce. Their setup is built to show cost per job by city. Right now yours cannot.

2 — Comfort Windows & Doors: Google And Meta Running Together
Their Oneida page, as published: Title: "Oneida Roofing Company | 0% Financing for 12 Months" H1: "Oneida Roofing Company" Then: Roof Replacement · Efficient Roof Repairs in Oneida · Roof Inspections "Request a Free Roof Project Estimate Today" On their homepage: 4 Google Ads conversion tags, Tag Manager, and a Meta pixel. In the Ad Library: 22 live ads, 18 of them testing multiple versions.
What their Meta ads actually say
  • Local weather hook: "New York weather doesn't play nice — your roof shouldn't either."
  • County call-out: "We're looking for homeowners in OSWEGO COUNTY for a home siding transformation!"
  • Seasonal urgency: "Save big this summer and be ready for when cold winter weather hits!"
  • Limited-time offer on individual products, and a 0% financing headline on every city page.
  • They even recruit on Meta: "Your toolbox deserves a better company. Call or text…"

This is the one to watch. They are a windows company that moved into roofing, they are on both channels, they are measuring both, and they have already named Oneida. The county call-out is worth copying outright: naming the county stops the scroll for exactly the people you can serve.

3 — Erie Home: What Heavy Meta Testing Looks Like
Around 93 live ads, almost all testing multiple versions

Erie Home is a national company based in Toledo, Ohio, with location pages for Syracuse, Rochester and Albany, and far bigger than any local roofer. So do not copy the budget. Copy the method. They run dozens of variations of a handful of ideas and let the numbers pick the winner.

  • "Check your ZIP" — "The ACTUAL cost of a new metal roof may be more affordable than you think. Check your ZIP (it's FREE)."
  • Price comparison — "How much does a new metal roof cost vs. traditional shingles? Get the answer with your FREE price comparison!"
  • Curiosity — "Roofers Don't Want You to Know About These New Metal Shingle Roofs (Here's Why!)"
  • Payment objection, head on — "$0 down", "no payments for 90 days", "50% off installation".
  • Holiday timing — Labor Day versions went live the week before.
  • Proof in the offer — a 50-year transferable warranty and a 25-point inspection named in the ad.

Nearly every ad answers the same unspoken question: "can I afford this?" That is the question stopping most roof replacements, and most local roofers never address it in the ad itself.

Worth Knowing: The Google-Only Competitor
Melo Roofing, Syracuse

No live Meta ads at all when we searched. But on Google they are serious: two Tag Manager containers, call tracking, a roof cost calculator, and around forty city pages — including Oneida, Rome and Utica.

That is useful for you in two ways. It proves the city-page approach works without a Meta budget, and it means Meta in your area is less crowded than Google. Of the four firms here, only two are properly on it.

What We Would Take From All Of This
  • A landing page per service, per city, for every paid click. The Graves Brothers system. Start with Oneida, Rome and Utica, because that is where the others are already spending.
  • Separate tracking for each page. So every city and service reports its own cost per job.
  • Answer "can I afford it" in the ad. Financing or a clear price anchor, the way Erie and Comfort do.
  • Call out the county by name on Meta. The cheapest scroll-stopper in the set.
  • Several versions of every ad. Test, keep the winners, retire the rest.
  • Close the review gap first. Campany's 4.8 and Blue Bird's 5.0 sit next to your 4.3 on every one of these searches, and no landing page fixes that on its own.
