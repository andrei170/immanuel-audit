You said the concepts are the same even when the firm is further away. So we left New York entirely and looked at roofers in smaller snow-belt markets — Scranton, Wilkes-Barre, Green Bay, Youngstown.

Different states, different sizes, same three habits: a page for every town they want, proof or an more than one offer, tested against each other, and reviews put where the customer sees them first.

1 — American Remodeling Enterprises: Test Offers Against Each Other
Family-owned, Pennsylvania, since 1982 — 27 live Meta ads when we checked
Roofing offers running at the same time: A "🚨 $1000 OFF ROOF Replacement" + "Limited installation spots available" B "With a 4.7-star rating from 1,109+ Google reviews & 600+ 5-star Facebook reviews, we promise to do it right the first time." C "Interested! Click 'Get Quote' to get a free roofing estimate." Ads started: 31 Jul – 1 Sep 2026, new ones most weeks

They do use a discount — but never on its own. The $1,000 off runs next to a reviews ad and a plain free-estimate ad, and the discount itself is wrapped in limited spots and a request to feature the finished job. The market tells them which one brings in buyers.

You are running one offer, "$2,500 off", and you told us it pulls in the wrong crowd. The fix is not necessarily to drop the discount. It is to stop betting everything on it and put a proof-led version beside it.

The other thing worth copying is the project gallery. They publish finished jobs as their own pages, named by town — "roof replacement in Hegins, PA", "roof replacement in Orwigsburg, PA". Forty of them. At 55–60 jobs a month, you finish more jobs in a single month than they have gallery pages, and every one of those is a page for a town you want to show up in.

2 — Prestige Roofing: Make The Offer An Instant Price
Green Bay, Wisconsin, since 2015 — 611 pages, 227 of them for a town or area
What their Meta ads say: "Get a quote online and book your new roof in under 60 seconds." "Get your quote online, choose your installation date, and we'll handle the rest." Where the ad goes: their own online quote tool Site structure: /service-areas/wausau-wi/ /service-areas/rib-mountain-wi/residential-roof-replacement/ ...81 town pages, 146 town + service pages, 340 blog posts

Their ads do not offer money off. They offer a price, right now, without a salesman. (Veteran and senior discounts sit on the homepage, not in the ads.) Somebody who clicks "book your new roof" is further along than somebody asking about a grant — the lead-quality problem you described.

And the pages are built as town + service: not just "Wausau" but "roof replacement in Rib Mountain". That is the structure behind showing up in 11 cities instead of 2.

3 — Black Label Builders: Qualify In The Ad Itself
Wilkes-Barre & Scranton — a 71-page site with 34 town pages
Their one live Meta ad (running since 28 July 2026): "Stop throwing money away on a total roof replacement you don't actually need." "Don't get tricked into an unnecessary $15,000 replacement." Top of their homepage: "★4.9 from 60+ verified reviews" Instant quote page: "Type your address. See your price."

Sixty reviews, and they still put the number at the very top. You have 51, and yours are not on your homepage at all. Review count does not have to be huge to be used.

Their ad separates repair from replacement before anyone calls. You told us you want to tell a $1,800 repair from a $19,000 replacement before it reaches your team — they do the first part of that in the ad copy.

4 — Cover Pro Roofing: You Do Not Need A Big Site
Youngstown, Ohio — 31 pages, 15 of them for a town
/roofing-warren-oh/ /roofing-canfield-oh/ /roofing-niles-oh/ /roofing-massillon-oh/ ...15 town pages on a 31-page site Meta ads under that name: none found Runs on: LeadConnector — the GoHighLevel platform, the same one your CRM is built on

Half their website is town pages, and nothing else is big. No blog empire, no ad library full of creative. It is the Campany pattern you found yourself — cover the towns and let the free searches do the work.

The point for you: the town pages are the cheapest part of this. You do not have to become Prestige with 611 pages to stop being invisible in 21 of your 23 cities.

What We Could Not Check
Marked honestly rather than guessed
  • Anyone's ad spend, lead numbers, cost per lead or revenue. We saw public sites and public ads only.
  • What any of them bid on in Google Ads. We checked for the tag on the homepage, not their keywords or budget.
  • Whether the ads work. The Ad Library shows what is running, not what it produces. Ad counts are what the library showed on the day and change weekly.
  • Search rankings. Page counts are structure, not a ranking report.
  • Every good roofer outside New York. These four were chosen because they are in smaller snow-belt markets comparable to yours.

Different states. Same three habits.

Town pages, more than one offer, and reviews up front — the three gaps already in your plan.
