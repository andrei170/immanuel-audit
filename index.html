Finding 1 — Triggers Built, Conversions Missing
Read from your live Tag Manager container GTM-N26MJS4J, 3 September 2026
TRIGGERS in the container Click ✓ present Form submission ✓ present Link click ✓ present Element visibility ✓ present Timer ✓ present CONVERSION TAGS in the container Google Ads conversion ✗ none GA4 event tag ✗ none Google tag ✗ none Conversion linker only present — but it links, it does not count

Somebody set up the listening and never attached the recording. The container watches for clicks and form submissions, and there is nothing inside it that turns those into a counted conversion.

This is the single most valuable thing in this document, because it means your Google Ads account is very likely optimising against nothing, or against something loose like a page view. Google bids on what you tell it to value. If it is not being told which clicks turned into a phone call or a filled-in form, it will keep buying the cheapest traffic rather than the traffic that produces roofs.

You said you already have SEO, Google and Facebook running. The question we would want answered before spending another dollar is not whether the ads work — it is whether anything has ever been able to tell you.

Bring the account to the call. Ten minutes inside it will settle this either way.

Finding 2 — Your Commercial Page Sells To Homeowners
Word-for-word identical copy, twice on the homepage
Residential Roofing "We offer expert roofing services to ensure your HOME looks great and is protected from the elements..." Commercial Roofing "We offer expert roofing services to ensure your HOME looks great and is protected from the elements..." ← identical

The commercial roofing description is a copy of the residential one, word for word. It talks about a home, to a business owner, on the page meant to win commercial work.

Commercial roofing is the higher-value half of your trade — bigger jobs, planned budgets, repeat buildings, and a buyer who does not haggle the way a homeowner does. You are not asking for it in its own language anywhere.

It also costs you in search. Google cannot tell the two pages apart if the words are the same, so neither ranks properly for what it actually is.

This is a rewrite of one paragraph. It is the cheapest fix on the list and it points at the most valuable work you do.

Finding 3 — Two Google Tags, And A Question To Ask
What is actually loading on your homepage
GA4 property G-QW1MWGTX6M loaded directly on the page Google tag GT-M3S9J6XS a second, separate loader Google Ads AW-11456797553 Tag Manager GTM-N26MJS4J Meta pixel present

All five are live, which is genuinely rare. But they have been installed in two different ways — some hard-coded onto the page, some through Tag Manager — and that is how double-counting starts.

The question to ask whoever set this up: is anything firing twice? If a form submission is counted once on the page and once through the container, your reported conversions are inflated and every cost-per-lead figure you have been shown is understated.

We are not saying that is happening. We are saying nothing in the public code rules it out, and it is a ten-minute check inside the accounts.

Finding 4 — Your Reviews Are Excellent And Unattributed
Three real, specific, persuasive reviews — and no rating anywhere

These are good reviews. They name behaviour rather than adjectives: the crew “treated my home as if it were their own”, a repair “resolved within a week”, and a quote that came in at exactly “what he quoted his estimate at”. One customer liked the work enough to bring you back for their son-in-law's siding.

Star rating published none Review count published none Customer names shown none

No score, no count, no names. A rating with a number beside it is the fastest proof a stranger can absorb, and somebody arriving from a paid ad decides in seconds.

For a business paying for clicks, this is the cheapest conversion improvement available — the traffic is already bought and paid for by the time they reach this page.
