Why this outranks everything else in this document

You ran ten prompts — best roofer, roof repair, trusted roofer, local roofer near me — across twenty-three cities. 207 searches. You came back eight times, in two cities. Campany came back in eleven cities and 25% more often.

Campany runs no Meta ads at all. We checked the Ad Library: nothing. So the firm beating you across your own service area is doing it entirely on organic presence, with no advertising budget in the fight.

That reframes the whole conversation. Your paid problem sits on top of an organic problem — you are paying $16 to $22 a click to appear in cities where you could be appearing for nothing, and where a competitor already is.

One methodology note, so nobody is caught out: those results came from AI search prompts rather than a rank-tracking tool. The direction is unmistakable and it matches what you saw manually on the last call. Before any work is scoped against it we would re-run the same twenty-three cities through a proper rank tracker, so the baseline is one nobody can argue with.

And it is the cheapest gap to close

Twenty-one cities with no page, no presence and no ranking. A service-area page per city is a known, finite piece of work — not a campaign, not a monthly spend, and it does not compete for budget with anything else here.

It also compounds. Every city page you rank in keeps working after it is built, which is the opposite of an ad that stops the day you stop paying.

Why we are comfortable being paid this way

Because the thing being fixed first is measurement. A performance fee is only honest if both sides can see the same numbers — and right now your Google dashboard reports 244 conversions at the top and 5 leads underneath.

So the sequence matters: tracking is attached first, attribution is agreed in writing, and only then does a performance fee mean anything. We would not ask you to sign a revenue share against numbers nobody can currently verify, and you should not accept one from anybody who would.

One caution on the arithmetic, said plainly

On the call we worked through an example where an extra $1.5M of revenue at 1.5% produced about $23,000 on top of the base. That was an illustration of how the structure behaves, not a forecast. We are not projecting that figure, or any figure, and nothing in this document should be read as a prediction of what the work would produce.
